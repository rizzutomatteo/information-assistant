A question crosses eight layers: from the web chat to access control, the input guardrail, the
LangGraph\index{LangGraph} orchestrator~\cite{langgraph}, the sources, the generation and the
output guardrail, up to the answer or an operator. Login is optional: a guest gets general answers
from the public documents, a customer also gets answers on their own policy. Everything is free:
one server, plus Groq's free \acs{api} for the two large models (Figure~\ref{fig:overview-strip}).

\section{Components}
\label{sec:components}\index{policy server}\index{token exchange}\index{core insurance system}

Most components in Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} are standard pieces chosen for being free and
well documented; the one written specifically for this project is the policy server. It is a
server based on the Model Context Protocol\index{Model Context Protocol}~\cite{mcp} that stands in
front of the company's core insurance system and exposes only four read-only operations:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the list of the policies of the logged-in customer, with product, edition of the
    conditions, status and dates, and the insured object masked;
  \item the guarantees, limits, deductibles and premium of one policy;
  \item the instalments of a policy, with their due dates and status;
  \item the open claims of a policy, with their stage and the contact person.
\end{itemize}

Each operation protects the data in its own way. A customer only ever sees policies that belong to
the subject of the login token: asking for someone else's policy returns \enquote{not found}, rather
than \enquote{forbidden}, so as not to confirm that the policy exists. Bank details are cut to their
last four digits, and the claims never include reserves or internal notes. The token used by the
policy server is not the one of the chat: Keycloak issues a new one, limited to this purpose,
through the standard token exchange~\cite{keycloak2026tokenexchange}.

\subsection{Routing Rules}
\label{sec:routing}\index{confidence}\index{policy selection}\index{rewording}

Three decisions of the graph are plain rules, not model outputs, and their counters restart at
every turn.

\paragraph{Which policy?} A customer may hold several policies. The graph uses the one the customer
names, by its number or by the plate of the vehicle; otherwise, the only active one; otherwise, the
one discussed in the previous turn. If none of these applies, it asks the customer.

\paragraph{Is the context enough?} The reranker gives each retrieved clause a score. Clauses below
a minimum threshold are dropped, the others are kept, and those above a higher threshold are
considered strong. The confidence shown with the answer follows from them: it is high when at least
one clause is strong and medium when at least two are kept. Otherwise the context is too weak to
answer. The thresholds are calibrated on the golden set of test questions.

\paragraph{How many attempts?} When the context is too weak, the question is reworded with
insurance synonyms, such as \textit{furto} and \textit{sottrazione} (theft), \textit{franchigia} and
\textit{scoperto} (deductible), \textit{sinistro} and \textit{evento} (claim), and searched again, at
most twice. When a draft fails the output check, it is regenerated once. If the problem persists,
the conversation goes to an operator.
