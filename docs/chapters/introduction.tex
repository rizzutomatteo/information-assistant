A customer who wants to know whether a hailstorm is covered should not have to read dozens of
pages of conditions, nor wait for the office to open. This project builds an assistant that gives
that answer at any hour, in plain Italian, with its sources, and that knows when to call a person.
Anyone can ask about the company's products; customers who log in can also ask about their own
policy.\index{guest}\index{login}

\section*{Application Domain}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Application Domain}

Insurance customers ask the same questions again and again: what is covered, what is excluded,
how much they pay, which form to fill in, where their claim stands. The answers are spread over
FAQs, forms, support pages, the \acf{dip} and the \acf{cga}, long contractual documents that change
with every edition; for a customer, the numbers are in their own policy
(Figure~\ref{fig:intro-example}).

\section*{Motivation}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Motivation}

A language model can read those documents and answer in seconds, and a \ac{rag} system gives it
the right passages. But in insurance a fluent answer is not enough: an invented clause, a number
from the wrong edition or a promise of payment can cost the company a complaint, and the customer
a wrong decision. The assistant is designed around these risks (Figure~\ref{fig:intro-compare}),
and it does so at zero cost.

\section*{Problem Statement}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Problem Statement}

\begin{tcolorbox}[accentcard, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
  \cardtitle{\faBullseye}{Goal}
  An assistant that answers in Italian, only from the approved documents, citing every source:
  general questions for anyone, and questions on their own policy for customers who log in. It
  states its confidence, hands the conversation to a person whenever it cannot answer safely,
  learns from the operators' corrections, and costs nothing to run.
\end{tcolorbox}
